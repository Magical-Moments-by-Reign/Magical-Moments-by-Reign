## 1. Education Purpose

A shared engine that teaches members **what they need to know before major life
decisions** — across every Estate — so choices are informed, timely, and confident. It
explains: what options exist · what questions to ask · what decisions are coming · what
documents may be needed · what deadlines matter · what risks to consider · which official
or licensed sources to consult · and how education flows into planning, tools,
professionals, and progress.

It **reduces confusion without pretending to replace licensed professionals.**

---

## 2. Education Philosophy

Never a textbook — a **trusted concierge calmly explaining**: what this means · why it
matters · what to do next · what to ask a professional · where to verify it.

- **Warm, plain-language, honest.** Approachable for a beginner, still useful for an expert.
- **Decision-oriented.** Every topic ends in a next step, not "read this article" (§13).
- **Trust-first.** Neutral, sourced, current, jurisdiction-aware, and clearly bounded from
  regulated advice. Inherits the constitution's **no-fabrication** rule absolutely.

---

## 3. Shared Learning Structure

Every learning topic inherits the **same reusable skeleton** (adapted by Estate/topic; not
every section is used every time):

1. Plain-language overview  2. Why this matters  3. Who this applies to  4. Common options
5. Benefits & tradeoffs  6. Questions to ask  7. Documents commonly needed  8. Deadlines/
timing  9. Common mistakes  10. Warning signs  11. Official resources  12. When
professional help is appropriate  13. Related tools/calculators  14. Related checklist
15. Related professional categories  16. Next recommended step.

This mirrors the Framework's Learning Center module and plugs directly into Checklists,
Tools, Documents, Professionals, and Progress via sections 13–16.

## 5. Provenance & Trust

Every educational item carries a **trust record**: author/owner · source type · official
source link(s) · jurisdiction · state/local applicability · effective date · last-reviewed
date · review frequency · professional reviewer (where required) · version history · archive
status · confidence level · disclaimer type.

**Content is never shown as "current" if it hasn't been reviewed.** Items past their
review date are visibly marked *pending review* or archived (§15).

**Content classes (clearly labeled to members):**

| Class | Trust weight | Concierge may present as guidance? |
|-------|--------------|-------------------------------------|
| Official government guidance | Highest (with source link) | Yes, cite & link the authority |
| Licensed-professional-reviewed | High | Yes, within jurisdiction/date |
| General educational | Medium | Yes, clearly educational |
| Partner-provided | Conditional | Only with disclosure & labeling |
| User-generated | Low | Not as trusted guidance |
| **AI-generated draft** | **Untrusted until reviewed** | **Never published as trusted guidance without human/professional review** |

## 6. Jurisdiction Handling (state & local variation)

Many topics differ by **state, county, school district, municipality, lender, insurer, or
agency** — e.g. graduation requirements, dual enrollment, marriage licenses, homebuyer
assistance, construction permits, landlord rules, short-term-rental regulations, funeral/
burial requirements, insurance requirements, professional licensing, tax deadlines, vehicle
rules, business registration, military/veteran resources.

**Engine rules:**
- Content is tagged with **jurisdiction scope** (national / state / county / municipal /
  agency). The member's location (with consent) selects the right variant.
- **Never present one jurisdiction's rules as universal.** A national overview is always
  labeled as general; specifics are labeled with their jurisdiction and effective date.
- **When exact local guidance is unavailable**, say so plainly and route to the official
  source: *"We have a general guide; your county's specifics aren't confirmed yet — here's
  the official agency to verify."* (See §17.)

## 7. Age-Appropriate Education

Content adapts across bands — **young children · older children · teenagers · young adults ·
adults · older adults · caregivers · parents/guardians** — in language, depth, and scope,
without oversimplifying high-stakes content into something misleading.

**Example — first bank account:**
- *Teenager receives:* checking vs. savings · debit-card basics · fraud awareness ·
  budgeting · first paycheck · questions to ask at the bank.
- *Parent receives:* custodial-account options · documents commonly required · permission &
  ownership considerations · questions for the financial institution.

**Guardrails:** minors' content is guardian-scoped; regulated topics stay educational and
defer to adults/professionals; accessibility-forward for older adults (§18).

## 8. Learning Paths

A **guided sequence** assembled from: Estate · goal · current stage · age · state · previous
knowledge · deadlines · risk level · professional involvement · member preferences. Paths
**advance as the member advances** (completed steps recede; the next relevant step surfaces).

- Paths are **configuration** (per the Framework's `learningPaths`), not bespoke code.
- The concierge recommends the path and the *next* lesson within it (§10).
- Deadlines and jurisdiction can reorder/insert steps (e.g. a looming tax or scholarship
  date is pulled forward).

*(Concrete example paths in §21.)*

---

## 9. Knowledge Assessment (optional, supportive — never a test)

Light, kind ways to personalize — never graded, never judgmental:
- "How familiar are you with this?" · short readiness questions · topic-confidence rating ·
  previous experience · goals & concerns · documents already collected · professional already
  selected.

Results tune the path's depth and starting point (skip basics for the experienced; slow down
for the new). Always skippable; framed as "so I can help better," not "let's see what you
know."

---

## 10. Concierge Integration

The concierge (per Step 4) uses education to guide. It may: recommend a topic · explain a
difficult term · summarize an **approved** guide · compare **approved** options · ask what
the member understands · suggest official resources · create questions for a professional ·
link education to tasks · to calculators · to documents · to **vetted** professionals · and
remind about changing rules or deadlines.

**Constraint:** the concierge may only use content **approved for the relevant jurisdiction
and member context**, and only what the requesting member is permitted to see. It never
turns education into regulated advice (defers per Step 4 §12).

---

## 11. Neutral Comparisons

Honest, side-by-side weighing of real options — e.g. 15- vs. 30-year mortgage · FHA vs.
conventional · college vs. trade vs. military · renting vs. buying · fixed vs. ARM · new
construction vs. existing · term vs. permanent life insurance · public vs. private college ·
Airbnb ownership vs. long-term rental · repair vs. replace.

**Every comparison includes:** assumptions · benefits · tradeoffs · risks · **costs the
member supplied** · unknowns · questions for a licensed professional — and **no hidden
partner preference.**

**Disclosure rule:** a partner's option is **never ranked higher for payment or sponsorship
without clear, visible disclosure.** Comparisons present options fairly; the member decides.

---

## 12. Calculators & Tools

Education connects to honest tools — e.g. mortgage payment · construction budget · home
affordability · closing costs · rental cash flow · flip budget · scholarship gap · college
cost · savings goal · wedding budget · travel budget · retirement estimate · vehicle
affordability · memorial budget.

**Every calculator must:** use member-entered or authoritative data · **state its
assumptions** · show formula/methodology where practical · avoid guarantees · explain
limitations · **save results only with consent** · route high-stakes interpretation to a
professional when appropriate.

Tools that require live external data (current rates, live market values) are **honestly
gated** until that data is real (§17); no fabricated inputs or outputs (constitution).

## 13. Education → Action

Education always leads to a next step — it **never stops at "read this article."**

- *After preapproval basics →* prepare a document checklist · compare lenders · save
  questions · set a reminder · connect a mortgage professional.
- *After dual enrollment →* check eligibility · contact the school counselor · review
  deadlines · add documents · create a student task list.
- *After funeral arrangements →* gather information · draft questions for the funeral home ·
  notify selected family · begin an obituary worksheet · organize service details.

Each "next step" hands off to a Framework module (Checklists, Documents, Tools,
Professionals, Notifications) with the member's confirmation for anything consequential.

## 14. Professional Review & Content Governance

Regulated/high-stakes topics require review by the appropriate professional before they are
trusted: **attorneys · CPAs · mortgage professionals · realtors · insurance professionals ·
medical professionals · mental-health professionals · funeral professionals · educators/
counselors · contractors/inspectors · financial advisors · military/veteran-resource
specialists.**

**Governance workflow:** review → approval status → expiration → re-review trigger →
conflict-of-interest disclosure → partner-content labeling → emergency removal → correction
process. Approved items carry the reviewer, jurisdiction, and dates in their trust record
(§5); expired reviews flip the item to *pending review* (§15).

---

## 15. Content Updates (never silently stale)

Content is monitored for change. **Triggers:** new law · new regulation · program expiration
· interest-rate-environment shift · school-requirement change · agency update ·
partner-policy change · broken official link · professional-review expiration · member
complaint · admin flag.

**Rule:** outdated content is **archived or visibly marked pending review** — never left
active while stale. Time-sensitive types (deadline alerts, state/local guides) have the
highest review cadence.

---

## 16. Search & Discovery

Members reach education through: Estate navigation · concierge recommendations · search ·
current stage · upcoming deadline · related task · calculator result · document request ·
professional connection · family invitation · notification.

**Search understands plain-language questions**, e.g.: "Can my child graduate early?" ·
"How do construction loans work?" · "What do I need to open my child's first bank account?"
· "What happens after someone dies?" · "How do I buy a rental property?" Results respect the
member's age, jurisdiction, and permissions.

## 17. Honest Empty States

Supportive, never fabricated:
- *No state-specific guide:* "We have a general guide, but Alabama-specific requirements are
  still being reviewed — here's the official source to confirm."
- *Content under review:* "This topic changed recently. Please verify the latest rule with
  the official agency while we re-review."
- *Official source unavailable:* say so; offer the general guide + the authority to check.
- *Reviewer pending:* label clearly as awaiting professional review.
- *Rule recently changed:* flag and route to the official agency.
- *No approved comparison:* offer the individual topics instead of a fabricated ranking.
- *Situation too specific / needs licensed advice:* "Your situation may require individual
  legal advice — I can help you prepare questions for an attorney."

**Never** invent a rule, a jurisdiction detail, a deadline, or a comparison to fill a gap.

## 18. Accessibility & Learning Preferences

Usable by the entire family: reading-level options · audio · captions · screen-reader
support · high contrast · larger text · short summaries · detailed explanations · multiple
languages · printable guides · save-for-later · family sharing (with permission).

Preferences persist per member (via concierge preferences, Step 4 §4) and apply across
Estates; the concierge honors them (e.g. always give short summaries first).

## 21. Example Learning Paths

**First-time homebuyer:** 1) Affordability → 2) Credit readiness → 3) Loan types →
4) Down payment → 5) Preapproval → 6) Realtors → 7) Inspections → 8) Offers → 9) Closing →
10) Homeownership responsibilities. *(Jurisdiction inserts local assistance programs;
deadlines pull relevant steps forward; each step links tools/checklists/professionals.)*

**Graduation:** 1) Graduation requirements → 2) Early graduation → 3) Dual enrollment →
4) College / trade / military / workforce pathways → 5) Testing → 6) Applications →
7) Financial aid → 8) Scholarships → 9) Enrollment → 10) Transition after graduation.
*(State-specific requirements applied; scholarship deadlines surfaced proactively.)*

Both paths use the **same** engine, structure, trust rules, and concierge behavior — only
content and configuration differ.
